yamul — pictographic glyph sketches v5 — single-glyph words

Each word is now one glyph. The iconemes that compose the word appear as simplified-scale sub-elements arranged in a regular layout — the way Chinese characters combine radicals into a single square block. The morphology is visible at a glance, but every word reads as one unified mark, not a strip of separate signs.

Each iconeme still decomposes into 2–6 brush strokes; sound-symbolic class (SHARP / SOFT) carries through stroke shape. Layout: a 2-iconeme word fills two cells side-by-side (head left, modifier right); a 3-iconeme word fills three cells; a 4-iconeme word fills a 2×2 grid. Stroke weight stays consistent at any scale.

When an iconeme appears as a component inside a word-glyph, it often uses a simplified radical form — fewer strokes, designed for the smaller cell. Compare Chinese 水 → 氵 (water as a left radical loses 4 strokes for 3 dots). For yamul, the densest iconemes — fon, kae, cur — get radical forms; minimal ones reuse their standalone shape unchanged.

word-glyphs

Each compound word renders as one glyph. Iconemes occupy fixed cells inside the block; the head sits in the leftmost cell, modifiers follow in head-first order. A 2-iconeme word is two cells wide; a 3-iconeme word is three cells wide; a 4-iconeme word fills a 2×2 grid.

example sentence

A short greeting written in the pictographic script. Each word is a single block (amber underline); the block's width tells you how many iconemes it carries.

, . .
semmedan, kaecurpe li. ku yo rowa kaecur coa.
“Good morning, sister. May you have a wonderful day.”
